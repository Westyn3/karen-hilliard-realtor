They share a school cluster, a five-minute drive to Avalon, and a price tag that starts near $1M — so why does the same buyer end up in one and not the other? Almost always, it's land. Milton's zoning preserves large minimum lots across much of the city, which is why you find equestrian estates, barns, and real privacy there. Alpharetta is denser, more walkable, and closer to the tech corridor and downtown.

The practical questions: Do you want a barn or a walk to dinner? Are you commuting to the 400 corridor daily, or working remotely? Do you want a gated golf community like The Manor or White Columns, a lake community like Windward, or acreage on Birmingham Highway? Each answer pulls you toward one city or the other.

My office is in Alpharetta, and I show both every week. The right answer is the one that fits how you'll actually live — and sometimes, after we've toured both, it turns out to be a lake house forty minutes north.
