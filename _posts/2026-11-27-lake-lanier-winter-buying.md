Everyone shops Lake Lanier in May, when the water's up, the trees are full, and every cove looks like a postcard. Smart buyers shop it now — because winter is when the lake tells you the truth.

**Winter shows you the water you'll actually own.** Lanier's level typically dips through fall and winter, and that's not a drawback for a buyer — it's free due diligence. The cove that looks perfect at full pool can be a very different story in January. Seeing a property at lower water tells you what the dock situation, the shoreline, and the usable depth really look like in the lake's leaner months. Buy the property that works in winter, and summer takes care of itself. (My [dock permit guide](/blog/dock-permits/) covers the rest of the water checklist.)

**Bare trees show you honest sightlines.** That "seasonal lake view" in the listing? Winter is when you find out what it actually means — and what your privacy looks like when the leaves are down, in both directions.

**Winter sellers are motivated sellers.** Lakefront owners who list in the off-season aren't testing the market. Fewer competing buyers plus serious sellers is the negotiating environment you want at lakefront prices — and it's how my clients have found some of their best water.

**And you're ready for spring.** Close in winter, and boat season starts on *your* dock. The buyers who start looking in April are still under contract when the lake fills.

I've lived on Lanier for more than twelve years, and winter is genuinely my favorite season to walk shoreline with buyers — it's the season with nothing to hide. If a lake home is in your plans for next year, this is the window to start.
