Land is the purchase where a good agent saves you the most money — because unlike a finished house, a lot's value depends entirely on what it can *support*. I've seen neighboring parcels in Dawson County that looked identical from the road and were six figures apart in real value. Before you offer on any land in North Georgia, get answers to these five questions.

**1. Will it perc?** Most land outside city limits here runs on septic, and a percolation test determines whether the soil can support a system — and how large a home it can serve. A lot that fails, or passes only for a two-bedroom system, is worth a fraction of one that passes clean. I make the perc test a contingency on [every land contract](/blog/perc-test/). Every time.

**2. Where's the water?** County water at the road is simple. A well is workable — but it's a real cost and a real unknown until it's drilled. Know which situation you're buying before you price the lot.

**3. What will the slope cost you?** Views come from elevation, and elevation comes with grading. A steep lot can demand an engineered foundation, retaining walls, and a driveway that costs more than a car. My construction background is exactly for this — I look at a lot and see the driveway, the septic field, and the foundation before I see the view.

**4. How do you legally get to it?** Road frontage, recorded easements, gravel versus county-maintained — access problems are the quietest deal-killers in mountain and rural land.

**5. On Lanier: is it *really* lakefront?** Dock rights belong to the property and are governed by the Corps of Engineers. A "lakefront" lot with no dock eligibility is a view lot at a waterfront price. I verify permit status before my clients write anything.
