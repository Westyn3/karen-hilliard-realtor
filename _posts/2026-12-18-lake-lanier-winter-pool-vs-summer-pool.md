If you've ever heard someone say Lanier is "full" or "down," it's worth knowing what they're measuring against. The Army Corps of Engineers manages the lake with two different full-pool targets:

- **Winter pool:** December 1 through April 30. Full is **1,070 feet** above sea level.
- **Summer pool:** May 1 through November 30. Full is **1,071 feet**.

That one-foot difference sounds small, but on a lake with miles of gentle shoreline, it moves the waterline more than you'd think. It also explains why the lake you tour in January can look different from the lake in June.

**What it means for a buyer.**

- *Dock depth is seasonal.* A dock that floats happily at 1,071 may sit differently at lower winter levels. Ask what the water is at the dock at full pool and when the lake is down.
- *Don't judge only on a good day.* If you tour at peak, ask for photos from a dry stretch. If you tour at a low, ask what spring looks like.
- *Your agent should check the numbers.* The Corps publishes lake levels, and I look at the level and seasonal depth for every property so you know exactly what you're looking at.

**What it doesn't mean.** Seasonal pool changes are normal. They don't mean a property is a bad buy. Lanier has gone through low-water years and recovered, and the cove you choose matters more than the month you shop.
