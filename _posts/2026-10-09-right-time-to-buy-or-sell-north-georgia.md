It's the question I'm asked more than any other — at closings, at the lake, in my inbox: "Is now a good time to buy or sell?" Here's the honest answer: the national headlines matter far less than your situation and your specific market. North Georgia isn't one market — it's a dozen.

**If you're selling.** The real questions are local: how many homes like yours are for sale in your area right now, how fast are they going under contract, and what's your competition priced at? A well-prepared home in a low-inventory pocket — and parts of Forsyth, Hall, and North Fulton stay tight — can sell strongly in any season. A home that needs work in a crowded price band needs a sharper strategy. That's a conversation about *your* street, not a national chart.

**If you're buying.** Waiting for the perfect market usually costs more than acting in a decent one. What matters is whether the monthly payment works for you, whether you plan to hold the home long enough for normal ups and downs to smooth out, and whether the right house is actually available. Fall and winter buyers in North Georgia often face less competition per listing than the spring crowd — sellers who list in November are typically serious.

**If you're doing both.** Selling and buying at once is a sequencing problem, and it's where a plan earns its keep — what you list for, what you can carry, and whether you buy first or sell first. I walk clients through that math before anything goes on the market.

**What I won't do** is tell you the market is always great. Sometimes my advice is "wait six months and do these three things first." You'll get the honest version.
