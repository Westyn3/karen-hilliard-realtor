"What's my house worth?" is the question I hear most from Gainesville homeowners — and the online estimate is usually the least reliable answer in Hall County.

Here's why. Automated tools work best in neighborhoods where every house is similar. Gainesville is the opposite of that. A home in [Harbour Point or Marina Bay](/gainesville.html) is priced by its lake access and dock situation, not its square footage. Two streets near the city line can feed different school systems — Gainesville City on one, Hall County on the other — and buyers pay attention. And on the water, the difference between deep-water frontage and a seasonal cove can be hundreds of thousands of dollars that no algorithm sees.

**What I actually look at.** When I prepare a valuation for a Gainesville seller, I pull the homes that genuinely compete with yours: same area, same school assignment, and — if you're on Lake Lanier — comparable water. I look at what's closed in the last few months, what's sitting unsold and why, and what buyers are actively searching for right now. Then I walk your home and adjust for the things a spreadsheet can't: condition, layout, the view from the back porch.

**What you get.** A realistic range, not a fantasy number — and a straight conversation about which improvements would actually return money before listing and which ones wouldn't. Sometimes the answer is "change nothing and list." Sometimes it's a week of small fixes. Either way, you'll know where you stand before you commit to anything.

**When to ask.** Earlier than you think. Most of my Gainesville and [Lake Lanier](/lake-lanier.html) sellers start this conversation six months to a year before they plan to move. There's no cost and no obligation — you'll just know your number.
