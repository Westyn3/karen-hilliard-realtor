Happy New Year! If "buy a lake house" is on your 2027 list, here's a simple way to make it happen so you're enjoying your own dock by boating season.

**January: Get clear on the life you want.** Year-round home or weekend retreat? Boat size? Gated community or private lot? Those answers narrow the map from "Lake Lanier" to a handful of specific areas. Browse the [lake communities](/lake-lanier.html) and note which ones feel right.

**February: Talk financing.** Lakefront and luxury purchases can involve different lending considerations than a standard home. Talk to a lender early. My [vendor list](/trusted-vendors.html) is a good place to start.

**February-March: Tour at winter pool.** Winter pool is 1,070 feet, and fewer buyers are out. It's a great time to see what you'd really own. I covered the seasonal levels in [winter pool vs. summer pool](/blog/lake-lanier-winter-pool-vs-summer-pool/).

**March: Do the due diligence.** Verify the dock permit, check the water depth, and review association rules and fees. See my [dock permit guide](/blog/dock-permits/) for the checklist.

**April: Make an offer and move quickly through inspection.** Properties that are right for you don't always wait. If you've done the earlier steps, you'll be ready.

**May: Summer pool begins.** The lake reaches its summer full-pool target of 1,071 feet on May 1. If you started this plan now, you could be closing close to that.

None of this is rigid. Some of my buyers move faster, some take a year. The point is to start the conversation before the spring rush, when the best properties are easiest to see and negotiate.
