Two lakefront homes can sit a few miles apart and offer completely different lake experiences. One of the biggest differences is whether you're tucked into a cove or facing the main channel.

**Coves: calm and protected.**
- Calmer water, with less wake and wind.
- Often better for swimming, paddling, and kids.
- Can be quieter and more private.
- Water depth can vary, especially toward the back of a cove. This is where checking seasonal levels matters most.

**Main channel: open and dramatic.**
- Wider views and more of the "big lake" feel.
- Faster access to open water.
- More boat traffic and wake, which can be rough on docks and boats.
- Often deeper water.

**Neither is better. It depends on you.** A family with young children may prefer a protected cove. A serious boater who wants fast access to open water may be happy on the channel.

**What to check either way.**
- *Depth at the dock*, in more than one season. See [winter pool vs. summer pool](/blog/lake-lanier-winter-pool-vs-summer-pool/).
- *Dock permit and condition.* More in my [dock permit guide](/blog/dock-permits/).
- *Exposure.* How does wind and wake affect the dock and your boat?
- *Shoreline condition.* Erosion matters. It's one reason the [Lake Lanier Association](https://lakelanier.org/) has installed riprap on about three miles of vulnerable shoreline.
- *Access to the rest of the lake.* How long does it take to get to your favorite spots?
