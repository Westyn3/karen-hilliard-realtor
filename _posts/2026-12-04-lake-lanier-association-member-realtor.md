When you buy on Lake Lanier, you're not only buying a house. You're buying into a lake, and the health of that lake is the biggest factor in what your property is worth and how much you'll enjoy it. That's why I joined the [Lake Lanier Association](https://lakelanier.org/), and why I'm proud to say so on my website.

**What the LLA actually is.** Founded in 1966 by a group of lake homeowners, the Association is a volunteer-driven nonprofit working to keep Lanier, in its own words, clean, full, and safe. It's now supported by roughly 4,500 members, and its work is practical, not abstract.

**What they do on the water.** If you've been on the lake, you've probably seen their work without knowing it:

- **Shore Sweep**, the annual shoreline cleanup. The 2025 cleanup was the 37th, with more than 1,200 volunteers and a record 128 tons of trash collected.
- **Water quality testing.** Volunteers test 27 sites around the lake every month for clarity, temperature, and algae levels.
- **Shoreline protection.** Through its riprap program, including 2025's Project Armor, the LLA has now protected about three miles of vulnerable shoreline from erosion.
- **Safety.** Life jacket loaner stations and solar lights on hazard markers.
- **Advocacy.** Speaking for lake users with the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers and other decision-makers.

**Why it matters when you're buying.** Water quality, shoreline condition, and lake levels are the things that make a lake home worth owning year after year. An agent who sells Lanier should care about those things, understand them, and stay close to the people working on them.
