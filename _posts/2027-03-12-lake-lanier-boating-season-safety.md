Warm weather is coming and the boats are about to come out. Lanier is one of the busiest lakes in the country, with the Lake Lanier Association citing over 12 million visits a year, so a little preparation goes a long way. Here's what I tell new lake owners.

**Wear a life jacket.** It's the simplest thing you can do. The [Lake Lanier Association](https://lakelanier.org/) maintains twelve life jacket loaner stations around the lake so guests and families can borrow one.

**Know the hazards.** Lanier's water level changes, and hazards can sit just below the surface in places. The LLA supports about 300 solar lights on hazard markers to help boaters see them.

**Check your dock before the season.** After winter, inspect flotation, decking, cleats, and electrical connections. If anything looks off, get it repaired before the first weekend.

**Mind your wake.** Your wake can damage neighbors' docks and boats. Courtesy goes a long way in a cove.

**Pack in, pack out.** Trash on the shoreline hurts water quality and wildlife. The LLA's Pack-In, Pack-Out message is one worth following.

**Know the rules.** Boating and shoreline regulations apply. Check current requirements before you launch rather than relying on old advice.

**Have a plan for guests.** If you're hosting, brief them on safety basics, including where life jackets are and how to call for help.

**If you're shopping for a lake home,** this is also a good time to see properties from the water. You'll learn things from the dock that you can't see from the driveway.
