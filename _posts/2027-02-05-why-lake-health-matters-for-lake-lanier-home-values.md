Buyers usually ask about the house, the dock, and the view. Fewer ask about the lake itself. They should, because the condition of the lake underlies everything else you're buying.

**Water quality.** Lanier is a drinking water source and a recreation destination. Groups like the [Lake Lanier Association](https://lakelanier.org/) coordinate monthly testing at 27 sites around the lake, tracking clarity, temperature, and algae levels. That kind of consistent monitoring is how problems get caught early.

**Shorelines.** Erosion puts sediment and nutrients into the water and takes away usable shoreline. The LLA's riprap program, including 2025's Project Armor, has stabilized about three miles of vulnerable shoreline. When you evaluate a property, the condition of its shoreline matters, and so does what's being done around the lake.

**Lake levels.** A full lake is a usable lake: docks work, coves are navigable, and the shoreline looks the way it does in the photos. Advocacy for keeping Lanier full is a core part of the LLA's mission.

**Cleanliness and safety.** The annual Shore Sweep cleanup drew more than 1,200 volunteers in 2025 and collected a record 128 tons of trash. Life jacket loaner stations and lighted hazard markers keep people safer on the water.

**What this means for you.** I'm not going to tell you that any single program changes a property's value. But a lake that's cared for is a better place to own a home, and the people who live on Lanier clearly agree, which is why thousands of them support the LLA.
