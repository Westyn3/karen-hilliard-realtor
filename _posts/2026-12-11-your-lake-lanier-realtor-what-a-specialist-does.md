Plenty of agents can sell a house that happens to be near a lake. Far fewer can sell *the lake*. If you're shopping for a lake home, here's what separates a Lake Lanier realtor from a general agent.

**They know the shoreline is regulated.** Lanier's shoreline is managed by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, and dock permits attach to the property. A specialist checks what a lot can legally have before you fall in love with it. (Start with my [dock permit guide](/blog/dock-permits/).)

**They read water, not just listings.** Full pool is 1,070 feet in winter and 1,071 feet in summer, and the lake doesn't always sit there. A good lake agent looks at depth at the dock across seasons and knows which coves hold water and which don't.

**They know the communities.** The lake is a patchwork of very different addresses: the gated marina communities near Gainesville like [Harbour Point](/harbour-point-lake-lanier.html) and [Marina Bay](/marina-bay-lake-lanier.html), the north-lake enclaves like [St. Michael's Bay](/st-michaels-bay-lake-lanier.html), and golf-and-lake communities like [Chestatee](/chestatee-lake-lanier.html). Each has its own amenities, fees, and quirks.

**They bring the right team.** Lenders, inspectors, and other local professionals who understand waterfront. My [trusted vendor list](/trusted-vendors.html) is a good starting point.

**They're part of the lake community.** I live on Lanier, and I'm a member of the Lake Lanier Association, because the lake's health is the product my clients are buying.

**They market the water.** For sellers, a lake specialist knows that buyers shop the view first and the kitchen second, and plans photography, drone footage, and timing around that.

If you're comparing agents, ask them three questions: How do you verify a dock permit? What was the lake level when you last showed a property? Which coves would you avoid, and why? The answers tell you quickly who actually knows Lanier.
