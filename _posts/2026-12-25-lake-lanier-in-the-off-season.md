Merry Christmas from the lake. This time of year, Lanier is quiet in a way summer visitors never see, and it's a good moment to talk about what lake life is like once the boats come out.

**The coves are calm.** Summer on Lanier is wake and weekend traffic. December is stillness: glassy mornings, empty docks, and a shoreline you can finally hear. For owners who live here year-round, this is the reward for the busy season.

**The views open up.** With the leaves down, many properties gain long water views they don't have in summer. It's also the honest picture of what a home's sightlines are. (Buyers: pay attention to this.)

**The house works harder.** A lake home in winter needs a bit more attention: checking the dock and any lift, making sure outdoor plumbing is protected, and keeping an eye on the property if you're a part-time owner. Good local professionals matter, and my [trusted vendor list](/trusted-vendors.html) is a place to start.

**Year-round or second home?** Some owners are here all year. Others use Lanier as a weekend and holiday retreat from Atlanta, Alpharetta, or beyond. Both are valid, and which one you are changes what you should buy. Proximity to GA-400, a gated community with management, or a home that's easy to lock up and leave all matter more for second-home owners.

**The community stays active.** Even in the off-season, people who care about the lake are working: shoreline projects, water testing, and advocacy continue year-round through groups like the [Lake Lanier Association](https://lakelanier.org/), which I'm proud to belong to.

If you've been dreaming about a home on the water, the holidays are a great time to start a quiet, no-pressure conversation. I'm happy to talk through what year-round or part-time lake life would look like for you.
