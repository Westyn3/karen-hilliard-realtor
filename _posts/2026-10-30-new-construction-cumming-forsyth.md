[Cumming and Forsyth County](/cumming.html) offer some of the best new-construction shopping in Georgia — nationally ranked schools, modern floor plans, master-planned amenities. But here's what every new-construction buyer needs to hear before touring a model home: **the builder's contract was written by the builder's attorneys, for the builder.** The sales agent in the model home works for the builder too. You're the only one at the table without representation — unless you bring it.

**What I read before you sign.** Builder agreements differ from standard Georgia contracts in ways that matter: how deposits and design-center money are held, what happens if the completion date slips, what the builder can substitute ("or equivalent" covers a lot), and how much room you actually have to negotiate on price versus incentives, closing costs, and upgrades. Some builders won't move on price but will move meaningfully elsewhere — knowing which lever to pull is the negotiation.

**Why my background helps here.** I came to real estate from construction management, so I don't just read the contract — I walk the build. Pre-drywall is the visit that matters most: it's the one time you can see the framing, plumbing, and wiring before they're sealed behind sheetrock. I attend those walks with my clients and I recommend an independent inspection even on new construction, because "new" and "correct" are not the same word.

**And one thing most buyers don't know:** in most cases the builder pays your agent's commission — meaning representation costs you nothing, but only if your agent is registered with the builder from your *first visit*. Walk into a model home alone and sign in, and you may lose the right to bring representation at all.
