Search for luxury homes on Lake Lanier and you'll see a lot of big houses. But on a lake, size isn't what makes a property luxury. Here's what I look at when I evaluate a luxury lakefront for my clients.

**1. The water.** The best properties have good depth at the dock, protected coves, and reliable access to open water. A beautiful home with a shallow, exposed shoreline is not a luxury lakefront, no matter what the finishes look like.

**2. The dock.** Permitted size, slips, covered or enclosed features, and lift capacity all vary. A high-quality dock with the right permit is part of the value. I always verify what the permit allows. (Details in my [dock permit guide](/blog/dock-permits/).)

**3. Privacy and lot.** True luxury often means room: a lot that gives you separation from neighbors, mature trees, and a gentle, usable slope to the water instead of a steep climb.

**4. The community.** Many of Lanier's most sought-after addresses are gated communities with their own amenities. Think private marinas, clubhouses, and in some cases golf. A few I work with often include [Harbour Point](/harbour-point-lake-lanier.html), [Marina Bay](/marina-bay-lake-lanier.html), [St. Michael's Bay](/st-michaels-bay-lake-lanier.html), and [The Reserve Club](/reserve-club-lake-lanier.html).

**5. The build.** Architecture, systems, and finish quality. My construction management background helps me look past the staging at how a house is actually built.

**6. The lake's long-term health.** A luxury lake home depends on a healthy lake. That's part of why I'm a member of the [Lake Lanier Association](https://lakelanier.org/), which works on water quality and shoreline protection.

**What about price?** Luxury lakefront prices vary enormously by cove, community, and dock. I'd rather walk you through real comparisons than quote a range here. See [my luxury homes page](/luxury-homes.html) for how I work with higher-end buyers.
